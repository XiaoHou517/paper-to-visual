# Personal Style: Vintage Sage Ink-Wash Collage

This profile is portable and does not depend on earlier conversations. The user's latest explicit preferences and reference artwork take precedence over implementation suggestions.

## Established preferences

- Green is the main color family. Related green tones may blend through washes and gradients.
- Both the presentation and poster need visible ink-wash effects, tactile texture, and a vintage atmosphere.
- A nearly white generic gradient lacks character; a full-page saturated dark-green painting is too heavy. Do not interpret a request for stronger visual identity as a request to darken everything.
- References favor aged paper, muted colors, detailed topic-specific imagery, watercolor boundaries, and layered scenes. Borrow these characteristics, not the references' people, universities, historical subjects, or brands.
- The paper texture and muted sage background were accepted during real use. For later typography feedback, preserve accepted background assets and concentrate on text and evidence.
- Text must feel purposeful and substantial. Avoid a tiny dense text block surrounded by large unused areas.
- Enlarging fonts and line spacing alone did not resolve sparse layouts. Rebuild information structure when needed, bringing back sourced explanation, evidence, and limits.

## Visual language

Use light aged ivory or gray-green paper with dusty sage, celadon, moss, and small areas of dark gray-green. Create vintage character through fibers, fine grain, subtle mottling, broken wet and dry strokes, and gentle fading rather than blackening or dirtying the entire page.

Ink wash integrates the subject into the surface: irregular diffusion, pigment speckles, fragmented strokes, and layered blooms at the boundaries. Within the subject, combine the research object with a few connected method or application motifs for narrative depth. The subject should remain recognizable in a thumbnail.

Freshness comes from translucent color, clear information, and breathing room. Classical character comes from paper, texture, and composition. Scientific imagery should supply any technology feel naturally; do not add default glowing circuits or grids.

## Suggested palette

These values are implementation starting points, not individually mandated colors.

| Role | Suggested value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aged ivory | `#EAE3D5` | Visible paper fibers and subtle mottling |
| Pale gray-green | `#CCD3BB` | Light washes and secondary layers |
| Dusty sage | `#9BA98A` | Main illustration midtones |
| Moss | `#73866A` | Structural details and selective strokes |
| Dark gray-green | `#354C40` | Text, fine detail, and small concentrated ink areas |
| Antique gold-brown | `#AE9470` | Optional restrained warm accents |

Keep the overall page light to medium in value. Use local detail contrast to clarify subjects without becoming uniformly pale or uniformly dark. Avoid neon and saturated emerald as the dominant field.

## Typography and composition

- Use a legible serif for cover titles when suitable, paired with readable sans-serif body text. Select fonts actually available in the host environment; do not imply font embedding without doing it.
- Make the title the first reading entry point. Group the title, explanation, and citation deliberately; do not scatter copy or let decorative imagery overwhelm the message.
- Content slides prioritize text and evidence. Align related headings, descriptions, charts, and conclusions. Use a few consistent alignment lines and purposeful group spacing.
- Differentiate titles, takeaways, body copy, and sources through restrained size and weight changes. Avoid uniformly thin text, excessive tracking, and body copy entirely in decorative serif typography.
- Sparse pages need adequate content area and comfortable line spacing. Use sourced relationships and explanation to build substance rather than stretching short sentences apart.
- Simplify illustration on dense scientific slides while retaining the shared paper and ink language.
- Recompose portrait and landscape independently. Avoid mechanically repeating the same splash in every location.

## Off-style outcomes

- Almost-white flat space with a small rectangular illustration and little thematic structure.
- A saturated dark-green field, neon accents, or harsh black-green contrast across the entire page.
- Smooth gradients, blurry circles, or regular wave lines replacing ink texture.
- Abstract splatter without a research-related central subject.
- Unrelated collage scenes, or default historical costumes, temples, logos, and portraits added solely to suggest vintage style.
- A few cramped lines surrounded by unused space, or exaggerated font sizes used to hide missing information design.

## Maintaining preferences

No fixed presenter name, institution, logo, or exact image-to-text ratio is specified. Their absence should not block ordinary authoring, and identities must not be invented. Save explicit long-term preferences here; keep one-paper facts and temporary styling choices in the task's design notes.
